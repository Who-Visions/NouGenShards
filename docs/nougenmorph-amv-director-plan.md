## Mission

Turn a user-provided, authorized music track and existing footage into an editable, frame-accurate music-video timeline. The system may analyze footage and propose edits; it must not generate replacement anime or invent source shots. A deterministic compiler turns reviewed decisions into a repeatable edit and render.

The governing boundary is:

```text
AMV = Render(VerifiedSources, FrozenAnalysis, TypedEditContract, AuthorizedPolicy)
```

Model inference is proposal-making. Rights checks, frame arithmetic, timeline validation, and rendering remain explicit and reproducible.

## Non-negotiable invariants

1. **Existing footage only.** Every selected shot resolves to an input asset hash and source frame interval. No generated anime or substitute footage enters the timeline.
2. **Rights are a hard gate.** `blocked` assets cannot be selected or rendered. `review` assets can be inspected in a private draft but cannot be exported. Only `authorized` assets can enter a release render, with a rights-evidence reference. Rights suitability is not a soft score term.
3. **Local, explicit inputs and zero additional spend.** No scraping or URL acquisition. Media stays in the chosen project root; the manifest stores project-relative references and content hashes, not ambient filesystem paths. No paid inference, paid APIs, cloud GPU rentals, new subscriptions, seats, or services. Existing local apps covered by Dave's Adobe Creative Cloud subscription may be used: After Effects first, with Premiere Pro, Media Encoder, and Audition staged for later. Do not assume the subscription includes separate cloud APIs or paid services. Prefer already available tools; verify licenses, transitive dependency costs, and hardware feasibility before choosing more. FFmpeg remains the free renderer fallback; Remotion remains optional only if it adds no charge. Hardware and electricity have operating costs.
4. **Observed versus inferred stays visible.** Beat samples, measured scene boundaries, model tags, human corrections, and editorial choices have distinct provenance, confidence, and version fields. Missing or timed-out analysis is `unknown`, never silently treated as “no matching scene.”
5. **Integer time at the editing boundary.** Use integer audio sample indices and integer frame indices. Store frame rates as rational numerator/denominator pairs. Frame ranges are zero-based and half-open `[in, out)`.
6. **Models do not own clocks or IDs.** A model may describe a supplied candidate shot and suggest an impact interval. Deterministic code maps verified sample/frame coordinates and enforces bounds. A model cannot invent a source ID, frame coordinate, or rights state.
7. **Determinism is scoped honestly.** Frozen analysis outputs, source hashes, config, contract version, and deterministic tie-breaking must reproduce the same edit manifest. Bit-identical render output is promised only for a pinned renderer/encoder profile; otherwise decoded-frame equality and timeline equality are the reproducibility target.
8. **No hidden delegation.** A fleet assignment is work only after that peer acknowledges and returns evidence. A green 4/4 route probe proves service reachability, not shared media, shard parity, or task completion.

## Proposed production contract

Keep the user’s proposed `SourceAsset`, `CandidateShot`, `MusicalAnchor`, `EditDecision`, and `AMVManifest` concepts, with these clarifications before implementation:

- `SourceAsset`: `sha256`, project-relative URI, explicit rights status and evidence reference, media time base, `fpsNum/fpsDen` for constant-frame-rate assets, duration, and probe/version receipt. VFR input is rejected in v0 or normalized to a deterministic CFR proxy with a retained source-time mapping.
- `CandidateShot`: `assetId`, half-open source frame range, action/character/emotion labels as evidence-bearing annotations, measured motion/energy values, optional impact frame **interval** plus confidence and annotator/model provenance. An interval preserves uncertainty better than a false-precision integer.
- `MusicalAnchor`: integer `sampleIndex`, `sampleRateHz`, kind, confidence, intensity, and source-analysis version. Model prose may label a phrase; signal processing provides its coordinates.
- `EditDecision`: asset hash and shot ID, half-open source frames, output frame range, target anchor sample and derived output frame, score breakdown, and rationale evidence IDs. The rationale is explanatory data, not executable instructions.
- `AMVManifest`: contract version, all source/audio hashes, frozen-analysis artifact hashes, model/provider/prompt versions where applicable, planner/config hash, deterministic tie-break policy, seed (fixed or unused for a non-random planner), output FPS rational, renderer/FFmpeg version and settings, and final output hash.

The anchor conversion must specify a single integer rounding rule. For sample rate `R`, output frame rate `F_n/F_d`, anchor sample `s`, source rate `S_n/S_d`, source in-frame `i`, and impact frame `p`:

```text
anchor_output_frame = round_even(s * F_n / (R * F_d))
impact_output_offset = round_even((p - i) * S_d * F_n / (S_n * F_d))
output_start_frame = anchor_output_frame - impact_output_offset
```

For v0, playback speed is fixed at 1.0. Speed ramps, optical-flow retiming, and variable-rate source clips are later work. If the computed output start or source range violates constraints, reject that candidate or ask for review; never silently clamp an impact to an unrelated frame.

## Editing objective

Treat rights, source bounds, timeline duration, and explicitly requested editorial constraints as hard constraints. Optimize only among feasible candidates. Use normalized, versioned soft terms for beat/section fit, energy, narrative semantics, motion continuity, shot variety, novelty, and transition quality. Provenance completeness can affect confidence and ranking, but cannot override the rights gate.

For a small library, feed the planner the complete validated scene table. For larger libraries, use a bounded retrieval stage that records query, candidate coverage, and omitted lanes; partial retrieval cannot be reported as exhaustive. Use a deterministic, bounded sequence optimizer (proposed default: beam search with fixed width and stable tie-breaks by score then asset hash/shot ID/frame). Compare it against a simple greedy baseline and a human-edited reference. This avoids copying a donor planner’s aesthetic or making undocumented quality claims.

### Acceptance gates

- Re-running the planner against identical hashes, frozen analysis, config, contract, and tie-break policy yields byte-identical canonical timeline JSON.
- Every edit resolves to an authorized source hash and valid source/output frame range; 100% of edits have provenance.
- Anchor/sample arithmetic is tested against synthetic fixtures at rational rates (including 24000/1001 and 30000/1001); measured anchor placement is within one output frame of the expected frame.
- Rendered frame count, FPS rational, duration, audio presence, and codec match the manifest under `ffprobe`; a second pinned render has matching output hash, or the manifest clearly reports that only decoded-frame equality is guaranteed for that backend.
- No model-only impact estimate is accepted as exact: low-confidence or wide impact intervals route to human review or a non-impact cut.
- Editorial quality is evaluated against a human-edited 30-second reference using blind preference ratings plus objective sync/repetition/coverage measures. Do not promote based on beat alignment alone.
- A timeout or missing analysis lane yields a partial/unknown report and blocks claims of exhaustive coverage; it may not silently lower the candidate pool.

Treat it as a benchmark hypothesis only; check its complete dependency/license chain and local feasibility. Its maintainer-reported cost/runtime are excluded from any acceptable deployment path.
