## 1. Tactical Intelligence & The 5 Disputed Theses

The debate centers on 5 structured prompts between Andrew Yang (the Realist) and 20 AI optimists/practitioners:

1. **"AI will not democratize opportunity — it will accelerate inequality on an unprecedented scale."**
   - *Optimist Stance*: Universal access to frontier intelligence levels the playing field; anyone with an internet connection commands senior developer/analyst capability.
   - *Yang / Realist Counter*: Access to the tool does not equal commercial capture of value. Capital, distribution networks, compute infrastructure, and proprietary data moats concentrate returns to the top 0.1% while commoditizing execution labor.

2. **"AI is going to eliminate millions of jobs faster than new ones can be created."**
   - *Evidence Cited*: Entry-level developer placement rates dropped precipitously (from historical 88% peaks down to 12% in specific cohorts); wages for junior software engineers declining.
   - *Historical Inversion*: Prior technological revolutions (steam, electricity, computing) created new labor classes over decades; AI automates cognitive white-collar tasks faster than human retubing cycles.

3. **"Universal Basic Income (UBI) will become necessary as AI reduces the need for human labor."**
   - *Economic Velocity*: Roosevelt Institute data cited: direct liquidity in consumer hands circulates locally, stabilizing demand. Without purchasing power distributed across the base, high-efficiency AI production faces a macroeconomic demand cliff.

4. **"The market will optimize for productivity and profit, not human dignity."**
   - *The Core Axiom*: The corporate mandate is margin expansion, cost suppression, and shareholder return. When autonomous agents replace headcount at $0.003/task vs $45/hour, fiduciary duty forces automation regardless of social fallout.

5. **"Being an AI optimist is necessary to survive the technological transformation."**
   - *Yang's Pragmatism*: Individual optimism ("agency") is required for personal survival, but institutional optimism is fatal negligence. Assuming "the market will work itself out" leads to widespread structural dislocation.
