# A theory of the dark matter — Daniel Friedan (arXiv:2203.12405v2, astro-ph.CO, 24 Mar 2022)
Note: this is a faithful condensation of the full 27-page paper (abstract, introduction, and the key results of sections 1-6, with the equations summarized in words and the paper's own numbers kept verbatim). The full paper is at https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.12405. Both arms of the benchmark receive this same document.

## Abstract

In an earlier paper the author proposed a highly symmetric semi-classical initial condition to describe the universe in the period leading up to the electroweak transition, completely determining all cosmology after that. Nothing beyond the Standard Model is assumed. Inflation is not needed. The initial symmetry allows no adjustable parameters. It is a complete theory of the Standard Model cosmological epoch, predictive and falsifiable. Here, the time evolution of the initial condition is calculated in the classical approximation. The fields with nontrivial classical values are the SU(2)-weak gauge field (the cosmological gauge field, or CGF) and the Higgs field. The CGF produces the electroweak transition then evolves as a non-relativistic perfect fluid (w_CGF approximately 0). At the present time, when H = H_0, the CGF energy density satisfies Omega_Lambda + Omega_CGF = 1. The CGF is the dark matter. The dark matter is a classical phenomenon of the Standard Model. The classical universe contains only the dark matter, no ordinary matter. At next to leading order the fluctuations of the Standard Model fields will provide a calculable, relatively small amount of ordinary matter such that Omega_Lambda + Omega_CGF + Omega_ordinary = 1.

## 1 Introduction

The initial condition is a classical SU(2)-weak cosmological gauge field (the CGF). The CGF cosmology has these attractive features: nothing beyond the Standard Model and General Relativity is assumed; there are no adjustable parameters; the initial symmetry and sufficiently large initial energy completely determine the initial state; the state of the universe is semi-classical and its time evolution can be calculated by a systematic expansion around the classical CGF; it is a complete and calculable theory of cosmology, predictive and falsifiable; it is an alternative to inflation, since the initial symmetry implies spatial isotropy and homogeneity, and a sufficiently large initial energy results in the observed flatness of the present universe.

The time evolution is calculated in the leading order, classical approximation, ignoring the fluctuations of the fields. After the CGF produces the electroweak transition, it drives the expanding universe as a non-relativistic fluid (w_CGF = 0) to become the dark matter in the present universe. The dark matter is a classical phenomenon in the Standard Model. The universe contains only dark matter in the leading order, classical approximation. The ordinary matter appears at the next order as a relatively small perturbation of the dark matter universe by the fluctuations of the Standard Model fields. The dark matter equation of state is derived in analytic form.

In a companion paper the Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff stellar structure equations for dark matter stars made of the CGF are solved numerically using the CGF equation of state derived here. The possible CGF dark matter stars — the solutions of the TOV equations — have mass M ranging from 0 up to 9.14 x 10^-6 solar masses, with radius R taking certain specific values between 5.23 cm and 13.6 cm. Presumably the CGF has collapsed gravitationally into an ensemble of such dark matter stars. For M greater than 5.09 x 10^-6 solar masses multiple values of R are possible, suggesting the possibility of transitions that would release gravitational energy on the order of 10^41 J in times on the order of 10^-10 s.

In another companion paper it is shown that the initial condition is thermodynamically stable under fluctuations of the SU(2)-weak gauge field, a first step towards showing that the initial condition is physically natural.

The classical CGF is the skeleton of cosmology — a universe containing only dark matter. The fluctuations of the Standard Model fields will flesh out the skeleton with ordinary matter. The skeleton has the right form. It remains to calculate the higher order corrections to check whether the CGF cosmology matches the observed universe.

## 1.1 Assumptions

The only assumptions are: (1) the universe is governed by the Standard Model and General Relativity (with cosmological constant); nothing beyond the Standard Model is assumed; nothing beyond the known laws of physics is assumed. (2) The universe is a 3-sphere. (3) The state of the universe is invariant under a Spin(4) symmetry group that acts on the 3-sphere as SO(4) and on the Standard Model fields such that the SU(2)-weak doublets transform as spinors. (4) The initial energy in the Standard Model fields is greater than 10^107 in natural units.

## 1.2 The CGF cosmology

These assumptions result in a unique cosmology. There are no adjustable parameters. The cosmology is semi-classical. The leading order approximation — the classical approximation — is solvable in terms of elliptic functions and elliptic integrals. The universe at leading order contains only the CGF, no ordinary matter. The ordinary matter appears at next to leading order as a perturbation due to the fluctuations of the Standard Model fields around the classical solution.

The only nontrivial fields in the Spin(4)-symmetric classical solution are the homogeneous, isotropic space-time metric characterized by the radius R of the 3-sphere universe, the Higgs field phi which is fixed by the Spin(4) symmetry at the local maximum phi = 0 of the Higgs potential, and a Spin(4)-symmetric SU(2)-weak gauge field (the CGF). The CGF is described by a single degree of freedom b(t), an anharmonic oscillator whose equation of motion is solved analytically by an elliptic function. The CGF oscillates between plus and minus k/epsilon in a quartic potential. The dimensionless energy E_CGF must be greater than 10^107 for the electroweak transition to take place and for the present curvature of the universe to be consistent with the observed flatness.

There is a natural parametrization of the scale of the universe, a = epsilon R, where epsilon = (8 E_CGF)^(-1/4). The local physics in terms of a is independent of the particular value of the energy E_CGF greater than 10^107, so the CGF cosmology has no adjustable parameters.

The universe expands, driven by the gauge field energy and the energy in the Higgs field at phi = 0. The CGF behaves effectively as a perfect fluid, a mixture of radiation with a small amount of vacuum energy, w_CGF approximately 1/3. The CGF oscillation is much faster than the expansion so an adiabatic approximation is justified, averaging over the CGF oscillation. The rapidly oscillating CGF contributes an effective phi-dagger-phi term to the Higgs potential which stabilizes phi at 0. The stabilizing effect weakens as the universe expands. The stabilizing effect becomes insufficient when the scale reaches a_EW = 0.585 hbar/m_Higgs. The electroweak transition starts. The Higgs field begins to move away from 0 towards its vacuum expectation value. The elliptic function b(t) is periodic in both imaginary time and real time. The imaginary time period defines an inverse temperature. The fluctuations of the fields are initially in the specific thermal state defined by this periodicity in imaginary time.

The initial condition leading up to a_EW is a specific semi-classical state of the Standard Model and General Relativity. All of cosmology after a_EW is calculable from first principles starting from this initial condition.

The adiabatic approximation continues to be valid after a_EW. The adiabatic equations of motion for the CGF after a_EW are solved analytically by elliptic functions and elliptic integrals. After a_EW, over the next 1 or 2 ten-folds of expansion, phi-dagger-phi tracks the minimum of the evolving effective Higgs potential to the vacuum expectation value v^2/2. The CGF evolves adiabatically to become harmonic oscillation at the bottom of the massive gauge field potential. From about 10^2 a_EW onward the expansion of the universe is driven by the CGF energy density rho_CGF = 0.890 m_Higgs/a^3. From about 10^2 a_EW onward the CGF is a non-relativistic perfect fluid — a perfect fluid with equation of state parameter w_CGF = 0.

The rate of expansion H decreases with time, reaching the Hubble constant H_0 at a_0 = 1.40 x 10^-8 s. The condition H = H_0 identifies the present. The present curvature is 1/(H_0^2 R_0^2) = 1/(H_0^2 a_0^2 (8 E_CGF)^(1/2)). The observed flatness of the universe puts a lower bound on the initial energy: 1/(H_0^2 R_0^2) < 0.001 if and only if E_CGF > 10^107. In the leading, classical approximation the only matter in the universe is the CGF. The present curvature is negligible so the present normalized energy density is Omega = Omega_Lambda + Omega_CGF = 1. There is only dark matter, the CGF. The fluctuations will provide the ordinary matter. The total energy density in the semi-classical state at H = H_0 will be Omega_Lambda + Omega_CGF + Omega_ordinary = 1. The ratio Omega_ordinary/Omega_CGF will have to agree with the observed ratio Omega_ordinary/Omega_CDM.

There are no adjustable parameters in the theory. The energy E_CGF, the one apparently adjustable parameter, is irrelevant to the local physics. Everything in the theory is calculable. Every calculation of an observable quantity is an opportunity to confirm or refute the theory. If the theory works, it will provide a first principles cosmology of the Standard Model epoch. And it will be a sharp target for theories of cosmology before the electroweak transition. A theory of the early universe will be successful if and only if it produces the Standard Model plus General Relativity in a spherical universe with Spin(4) symmetry and high initial energy.

## 2 Spin(4)-symmetric initial condition

Space is a 3-sphere. Spin(4) is SU(2)_L x SU(2)_R and acts on the 3-sphere as SO(4). Let Spin(4) act on the SU(3) and U(1) gauge bundles as product bundles, so the only Spin(4)-symmetric gauge fields are the trivial gauge fields. Let the SU(2) gauge bundle be identified with the spinor bundle over S^3. The only Spin(4)-symmetric Higgs field is phi = 0. There is a one parameter family of Spin(4)-symmetric SU(2) gauge fields on S^3. The cosmological gauge field (the CGF) is B_i^CGF = b(t) gamma_i(x), or for short, b(t). The general Spin(4)-symmetric classical state is characterized by the pair of functions R(t) and b(t) subject to the classical equations of motion. The Yang-Mills action is conformally invariant, so the action is independent of R. The field b is an anharmonic oscillator. The dimensionless energy E_CGF is conserved. The general classical solution (for E_CGF > 1/2) is the elliptic function b(t) = k cn(z, k)/epsilon, with z = t/epsilon, k^2 = 1/2 + epsilon^2, E_CGF = 1/(8 epsilon^4). The CGF oscillates between plus and minus k/epsilon in the quartic potential with period 4K epsilon, where K is the complete elliptic integral of the first kind.

## 2.2 Initial CGF energy

E_CGF is the only adjustable parameter in the classical solution. The paper starts out assuming E_CGF >> 1 so that the CGF is semi-classical. Later it is found that E_CGF must be very large if the cosmology is to be physically realistic. First E_CGF > 10^67 is needed in order that the CGF produces the electroweak transition in an expanding universe. Then a formula for the present curvature is derived: -Omega_curvature = 1/(H_0^2 R_0^2) = 1.07 x 10^51 epsilon^2. The observed flatness of the present universe, |Omega_curvature| < 0.001, implies epsilon < 10^-27 and E_CGF > 10^107. When E_CGF is very large, epsilon is very small, so to very high accuracy k^2 = 1/2 and K = K(1/sqrt(2)) = 1.854075...

## 2.3 Coordinates for local physics

Scale the spatial coordinates x = x-hat/epsilon to match the scaling of time z = t-hat/epsilon. The space-time metric in the scaled coordinate system is ds^2 = a(z)^2 (-dz^2 + g_ij(x) dx^i dx^j) with a(z) = epsilon R(t-hat). The local physics in the scaled coordinate system is independent of epsilon when epsilon is very small. Only global properties of the universe can depend on epsilon.

## 2.4 Start of the electroweak transition at a = a_EW

The Higgs field action in the presence of the CGF gives an effective action for phi with an effective Higgs potential. When a(z) is small the coefficient of phi-dagger-phi is positive so phi = 0 is stable. When the coefficient becomes negative, phi = 0 becomes unstable. The electroweak transition starts when the quadratic term vanishes, at a = a_EW given by a_EW = 0.5854 t_Higgs = 3.08 x 10^-27 s.

## 2.5 CGF density and pressure for a <= a_EW

The energy-momentum tensors give the CGF energy density and pressure. The first term in each expression is due to the gauge field, a perfect fluid with w_gauge = 1/3. The second term is due to the Higgs field vacuum energy, a perfect fluid with w_phi = -1. The equation of state for a <= a_EW is p_CGF = (1/3) rho_CGF - (1/(6 lambda^2)) hbar/t_Higgs^4.

## 2.6 Realizing the electroweak transition

The Friedmann equation shows the expansion passes through a_EW if epsilon^2 < 2.40 x 10^-33. The expansion passes through all values of a if epsilon^2 < 1.15 x 10^-33. So the paper assumes epsilon^2 < 10^-33, which is E_CGF > 10^67, in order that the expansion passes through a_EW and in order that the CGF oscillates for some considerable period before a_EW.

## 2.7 Adiabatic condition for a <= a_EW

To justify averaging over the CGF oscillation, the expansion time 1/H must be much longer than the oscillation period 4Ka for a <= a_EW. For a on the order of a_EW, the ratio of time scales 4KaH is on the order of 10^-16, so the adiabatic condition is well satisfied. For a significantly less than a_EW, the adiabatic condition is satisfied as long as a >> t_grav, so there is plenty of time for expansion up to a_EW with the adiabatic condition well satisfied.

## 2.8 Temperature from the CGF

The cosmological gauge field b(z) = k cn(z, k) is an elliptic function of the time z, periodic in real time with period 4K and also periodic in imaginary time with period 4K'i. The imaginary period in co-moving time defines an inverse temperature T_CGF. This is to be the origin of the cosmological temperature. The CGF acts as thermal bath for the fluctuations of the Standard Model fields. The correlation functions of the fluctuations are periodic in imaginary time with the period 4K'a i. This defines a specific semi-classical quantum state of the Standard Model plus General Relativity whose time evolution describes the universe from a_EW onwards. In a companion paper the initial thermal state of the SU(2) gauge field fluctuations is constructed and shown to be thermodynamically stable, a first step towards showing that the proposed Spin(4)-symmetric initial condition is thermodynamically stable and thus physically natural.

## 3 Mathematical literature

The SO(4)-invariant classical solution of pure SU(2) gauge theory on S^3 (without the Higgs field) was first discovered as a mathematical object by Alfaro, Fubini, and Furlan, Cervero, Jacobs, and Nohl, and Luscher. Ivanova, Lechtenfeld, and Popov examined properties of the SO(4)-invariant gauge theory solution on de Sitter and anti-de Sitter space. Lechtenfeld described the back reaction in General Relativity. The symmetry group was SO(4) in all of these works because Spin(4) symmetry can be posited only when there are SU(2) doublet fields such as the Higgs field. None of these mathematical works made or suggested any connection between the mathematical solutions and physical cosmology or the electroweak transition or thermal physics.

## 4 Adiabatic time evolution of the CGF

## 4.1 Oscillating CGF at a >= a_EW

As the scale a(z) approaches a_EW the fluctuations of the Higgs field around phi = 0 grow large. After a_EW the Higgs field phi fluctuates in and around the minima of the effective potential. As a(z) increases slowly, the potential well deepens and the phi fluctuations concentrate at phi-dagger-phi = (phi-dagger-phi)_0. The phi fluctuations are Spin(4)-symmetric so the effective action remains Spin(4)-symmetric. The CGF continues to evolve as a Spin(4)-symmetric SU(2) gauge field b(z) gamma_i but now with an effective action. After a_EW the CGF b(z) oscillates in a quartic potential whose quadratic term is changing very slowly compared to the oscillation. The dimensionless conserved energy is E_CGF = epsilon^4 E-hat_CGF. Again the oscillating solution for fixed mu and E_CGF is given by an elliptic function. After a_EW the parameters mu, E_CGF and k, alpha evolve slowly away from their values at a_EW (mu_EW = 0, E_CGF,EW = 1/8, k_EW^2 = 1/2, alpha_EW = 1).

## 4.2 Adiabatic invariant

The adiabatic invariant I = (1/2 pi) times the integral of p dq over one oscillation is a constant of motion in the adiabatic time evolution of an oscillating classical degree of freedom. Using the action, the adiabatic constant of motion is I-tilde = (1/alpha^3) [(1 - k^2) K + (2 k^2 - 1) E]. At a = a_EW, k_EW^2 = 1/2 and alpha = 1, so I-tilde = (1/2) K_EW. So the adiabatic equation is (1/alpha^3) [(1 - k^2) K + (2 k^2 - 1) E] = (1/2) K_EW. Now there are enough equations to parametrize the time evolution by k^2.

## 4.3 a as a function of the elliptic parameter k^2

The adiabatic equation gives alpha as a function of k^2. Then the other equations give mu^2, E_CGF, and the average of b^2 as functions of k^2, and then a as a function of k^2. Numerical calculations show that a increases monotonically from a_EW to infinity as k^2 decreases from 1/2 to 0. So the parametrization by k^2 implicitly gives the time evolution as a function of the scale a. The k^2 -> 0 regime is reached after about two ten-folds of expansion from a_EW.

## 4.4 CGF density and pressure after a_EW

For a >= a_EW, the energy-momentum tensors give the CGF density rho_CGF and pressure p_CGF as functions of E_CGF, mu^2, and the average of b^2, all parametrized by k^2. The dimensionless density and pressure are rho-hat_CGF and p-hat_CGF. These two equations parametrize the density and pressure by k^2. The equation of state relating p to rho is given implicitly by the two equations.

## 5 CGF as the dark matter

## 5.1 Omega_CGF + Omega_Lambda = 1 in the present

The Friedmann equation is H^2 + 1/R^2 = (1/3) kappa (rho_m + rho_Lambda). Ignoring fluctuations, the entire matter density is rho_m = rho_CGF. The Friedmann equation normalized by H_0^2 is H^2/H_0^2 = Omega_CGF + Omega_Lambda + Omega_curvature. The observed present curvature is close to zero, |Omega_curvature| < 0.001. The observed dark energy density is Omega_Lambda = 0.685. The dark energy is assumed due to the cosmological constant, so Omega_Lambda is constant in time. Using the k -> 0 asymptotic formulas, Omega_CGF = (K_EW/(6 pi g lambda)) (t_Hubble^2 t_grav^2)/(t_Higgs a^3). The present time is identified by the condition H = H_0. By the Friedmann equation, H = H_0 is equivalent to Omega_CGF = 0.315 assuming |Omega_curvature| < 0.001. So the present values of k^2 and a are k_0^2 = 7.89 x 10^-56 and a_0 = 1.40 x 10^-8 s = 2.66 x 10^18 t_Higgs = 4.54 x 10^18 a_EW. k_0^2 is very small so using the asymptotic formulas is justified. The present curvature is -Omega_curvature = 1.07 x 10^51 epsilon^2, so the present flatness condition, |Omega_curvature| < 0.001, is equivalent to epsilon < 10^-27 which is E-hat_CGF > 10^107. Then Omega_CGF + Omega_Lambda = 1. The CGF is the dark matter.

## 5.2 w_CGF approximately 0 after 10^2 a_EW

The equation of state parameter is w_CGF = p_CGF / rho_CGF. The formulas of Table 2 give, in the k -> 0 regime, w_CGF = (3/8 - 3 g^2/(64 lambda^2)) k^2, so the CGF evolves as a non-relativistic fluid (w = 0) after the first one or two ten-folds of expansion from a_EW. In the k -> 0 regime the formulas give rho_CGF = (K_EW/(2 pi g lambda)) (hbar/t_Higgs) (1/a^3) = 0.890 m_Higgs/a^3, showing the 1/a^3 behavior of a w = 0 fluid, which follows from the conservation of energy-momentum.

## 5.3 The CGF in the present

The dimensionful gauge field is B_CGF,i^phys = (b(z)/a) gamma_i = (k cn(u, k)/(alpha a)) gamma_i. In the k -> 0 regime (a > 10^2 a_EW), alpha a = 2 lambda t_Higgs/g = hbar/m_W = 1/omega_W and cn(u, k) = cos(u) = cos(omega_W t), so the dimensionful CGF is B_CGF,i^phys = k omega_W cos(omega_W t) gamma_i with omega_W = m_W/hbar = 1.22 x 10^26 s^-1. The present CGF oscillates harmonically at the bottom of the massive gauge field potential. The oscillation period is 4 K alpha a = 2 pi/omega_W = 5.15 x 10^-26 s. The present value of k is k_0 = 2.81 x 10^-28, so presently B_CGF,i^phys = 2.26 x 10^-26 (GeV/hbar) cos(omega_W t) gamma_i. Again, this is a leading order calculation, without fluctuations. Higher order effects presumably cause the fluctuating CGF to collapse gravitationally into an ensemble of self-gravitating bodies such as the dark matter stars described in the companion paper.

## 5.4 CGF equation of state

The density scale of the CGF is rho_b = hbar/t_Higgs^4 = 5.68 x 10^28 kg/m^3. The dimensionless CGF density and pressure are rho-hat_CGF = rho_CGF/rho_b and p-hat_CGF = p_CGF/rho_b. The dimensionless density at a_EW is rho-hat_EW = 7.97. The equation of state for rho-hat >= rho-hat_EW is p-hat = (1/3)(rho-hat - c_b rho-hat_EW) with c_b = 0.243. For rho-hat <= rho-hat_EW the equation of state is given implicitly by the analytic functions of Table 2. The equation of state is well-defined because rho-hat_CGF(k^2) is monotonic in k^2. The limit k^2 -> 0 is rho-hat -> 0. From Table 2, the equation of state in the limit is rho -> 0, p = (c_a/2) rho^2 with c_a = 0.992.

## 5.5 Adiabatic condition for a >= a_EW

Finally, it is verified that the adiabatic condition is satisfied for a >= a_EW, so the adiabatic approximation of the time evolution is justified. The ratio of the oscillation period 4 K alpha a to the expansion time 1/H is 4 K alpha a H. At a_EW the bound is 3.64 x 10^-16. The ratio of time scales 4 K alpha a H decreases monotonically from 10^-16 as a increases from a_EW. The present ratio of time scales is 4 K_0 alpha_0 a_0 H_0 = 1.12 x 10^-43. The adiabatic condition is well satisfied from a_EW onward (and, as shown earlier, for some ten-folds before a_EW). The adiabatic approximation of the CGF time evolution is justified.

## 6 Questions and comments

## 6.1 Testing the CGF cosmology

Two approaches to checking the theory seem obvious. (1) The initial thermal state of the fluctuations should be constructed and its time evolution calculated to see if the right amount of ordinary matter results. As a start, methods are developed in the companion paper to construct the initial thermal state of the fluctuations. (2) If the CGF is indeed the dark matter, can it be detected? What form does it take in the present? What are its interactions with ordinary matter? It seems reasonable to suppose that fluctuations in the CGF have collapsed gravitationally to stable self-gravitating structures. As a first step, the Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff stellar structure equations for stars made of the CGF are solved numerically in the companion paper. It should be possible to model a purely dark matter universe populated with galaxies of dark matter stars. The actual universe would be a perturbation of the dark matter universe. If the theory survives testing then first principles cosmology will become possible. All of the Standard Model cosmological epoch will be calculable from first principles. Eventually, discrepancies between the theory and cosmological observation can become clues to more fundamental physical principles.

## 6.2 Spin(4) and fluctuations

The Spin(4) symmetry is a global symmetry of the universe in the CGF cosmology. Presumably we live in a fluctuation where the global Spin(4) is not apparent. We see the Poincare symmetry of the Standard Model with the Higgs field phi transforming as a scalar, not a spinor. Before the electroweak transition, as the universe expands towards a_EW, the fluctuations of phi around 0 grow large. After a_EW the phi fluctuations concentrate again, now at the bottom of the effective Higgs potential, the set phi-dagger-phi = (phi-dagger-phi)_0 which evolves slowly towards the vacuum expectation value v^2/2. Spin(4) continues to be a global symmetry of the universe. The fluctuations of phi(x) at the bottom of the effective Higgs potential are Spin(4)-symmetric. The crucial quantity is the correlation length of the phi fluctuations after the electroweak transition. This is the largest distance xi such that the phi correlation function equals v^2/2 for separations below xi. The Standard Model as we observe it operates within regions of size xi.

## 6.3 Thermalization before a_EW

The imaginary time periodicity of the CGF defines a specific thermal state of the fluctuations of the Standard Model fields. That state is thermodynamically stable against fluctuations of the SU(2) gauge field. It remains to be checked that it is stable against all fluctuations. A generic state presumably thermalizes to this state. Is it possible to determine how long the thermalization takes? How long before a_EW the CGF must have been oscillating to ensure that the fluctuations are in the thermal state at a_EW?

## 6.4 CGF temperature after a_EW

The author's first motivation for formulating the CGF cosmology was to explain the origin of cosmological temperature as the imaginary time periodicity of the CGF. A handwaving estimate of the redshift a_0/a_EW was made by assuming that the CGF temperature T_CGF at a_EW redshifted to the CMB temperature T_CMB in the present. It can be made more definite now that the time evolution of the CGF after a_EW is known. The imaginary period in co-moving time t is Delta t = 4 K' alpha a i, so the inverse CGF temperature after a_EW is hbar/(k_B T_CGF) = 4 K' alpha a. The present CGF temperature is T_CGF,0 = 3.60 x 10^12 K with k_B T_CGF,0 = 310 MeV, so the ordinary matter must have decoupled from the CGF some time in the past. In the k -> 0 regime, T_CGF = 2.33 x 10^14 K / ln(4/k) with k_B T_CGF = 20.1 GeV / ln(4/k). Redshifting the asymptotic CGF temperature to the present gives (a/a_0) T_CGF = 8.77 x 10^-5 K (a-hat/ln(4/k)). The redshifted CGF temperature will equal the CMB temperature, (a/a_0) T_CGF = T_CMB = 2.7255 K, when a-hat is approximately 10^6, which is well within the asymptotic k -> 0 regime. Numerical solution finds k^2 = 5.12 x 10^-18, a/a_EW = 1.13 x 10^6, k_B T_CGF = 0.944 GeV. The CGF has to decouple from the ordinary matter at a = 10^6 a_EW when k_B T_CGF = 1 GeV. This is a very simplistic calculation. It ignores fluctuations and supposes that all of the ordinary matter decouples at the same time and that the temperature simply redshifts from that time on.

## 6.5 CP violation

If the CGF cosmology is to succeed as a complete theory of the Standard Model epoch, it will have to explain the baryon-antibaryon asymmetry. Everything after a = a_EW is completely determined by the Spin(4)-symmetric initial condition leading up to the electroweak transition. What comes before the Standard Model epoch is immaterial. The author's thought was that the discrete CP symmetry takes b-hat -> -b-hat (because the Dirac matrices gamma_i change sign when the orientation of space is reversed). During the transition b-hat settles in one of the minima b-hat = +/- 1 of the double well potential, breaking CP as it settles. But the two minima are indistinguishable in local coordinates, i.e. b = +/- epsilon. So any CP violating effects have to be global. The Higgs field fluctuations play a role in this. The symmetry group Spin(4) is SU(2) x SU(2). The Higgs field phi(x) decomposes into a sum of irreducible representations. CP exchanges (j + 1/2, j) with (j, j + 1/2). The orientation of the fluctuation in the vector space (j + 1/2, j) + (j, j + 1/2) can break CP. Again, any effects will be global since (j + 1/2, j) and (j, j + 1/2) become indistinguishable when j is large. The expectation value of the baryon number density will be zero. The question again is whether the baryon number density is correlated over large enough regions and whether the magnitude of the two point function in such regions is big enough.

## 6.6 Semi-classical approximation

A quantitative measure of the validity of the semi-classical approximation is the adiabatic invariant I. An oscillating quantum system is semi-classical when (1/hbar) I >> 1. For the CGF, the equations and the bound on E-hat_CGF give (1/hbar) I > 10^82. So the CGF is semi-classical.

## 6.7 Neutrinos

It is not entirely accurate that the theory assumes nothing beyond the Standard Model. This is true in the classical approximation. When fluctuations are taken into account, a mechanism will have to be added to the Standard Model to produce neutrino masses and mixing.
